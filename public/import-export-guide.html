India

Import Export Code (IEC)

The mandatory 10-digit license for all Indian international trade, issued by DGFT

The IEC is the single most important license for any Indian business engaged in cross-border trade. Without it, you cannot legally export or import goods. It is issued by the Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT) under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry.

Authority
DGFT — Ministry of Commerce
Application Fee
Rs. 500 (online)
Validity
Lifetime (annual profile update needed Apr–Jun)
Processing Time
1 to 7 working days
Official Portal
www.dgft.gov.in ↗
Code Format
10-digit alphanumeric number

Who Needs IEC?

Any individual or business entity importing or exporting goods from India
E-commerce sellers exporting through Amazon Global, Flipkart, or similar platforms
Manufacturers looking to sell products internationally
Merchant exporters and trading companies
Service exporters receiving foreign remittances above threshold

Exemption: Persons importing/exporting goods for personal use not connected with trade or manufacturing are exempt.

Step-by-Step IEC Registration

Go to the official DGFT portal: www.dgft.gov.in

Click on 'Services' tab → select 'IEC Profile Management'

Click 'Apply for IEC' and create a new account with email and mobile

Verify your email and mobile number via OTP

Log in and fill the IEC application form — business name, PAN, address, incorporation date

Enter bank account number, IFSC code, and account holder name

Upload all required documents in PDF or JPEG format (must be clear and legible)

Pay the Rs. 500 application fee online via net banking, UPI, or credit/debit card

Submit the application — you will receive an acknowledgement number

IEC certificate is issued digitally within 1–7 working days

Download your IEC certificate (contains QR code for online verification) from 'IEC Profile Management'

AD Code — Also Required

In addition to IEC, you need an Authorized Dealer (AD) Code from your bank. This enables customs clearance and foreign exchange transactions. It must be registered at each port you export from.

Visit your bank branch and request AD Code registration
Submit IEC certificate, bank account details, and business documents
Bank registers your AD Code with the customs department
AD Code has lifetime validity unless you change your bank or branch

Mandatory for exporters with annual turnover above Rs. 20 lakhs (Rs. 10 lakhs for special category states). Exports are zero-rated under GST, allowing you to claim full refunds on input taxes.

United States

USA Import & Export Licensing

No single universal license — requirements depend on your product and the agencies that regulate it

Unlike India, the USA does not have one universal import/export license. Most goods do not require a license. However, specific product categories require licenses or permits from relevant federal agencies. US Customs and Border Protection (CBP) enforces all trade regulations at US ports of entry.

Step 1 — Get Your EIN (Employer Identification Number)

The EIN is your business's federal tax ID issued by the IRS. It is required for all commercial import/export activities in the USA and must appear on all CBP entry forms.

Go to: www.irs.gov → search "Apply for EIN online"

Select your entity type (LLC, Corporation, Sole Proprietor, etc.)

Complete the online application — takes 10–15 minutes, completely free

EIN is issued immediately upon completion — save your confirmation letter

Use EIN on all CBP forms, invoices, and export filings

Step 2 — Register with CBP (ACE Portal)

The Automated Commercial Environment (ACE) is the US government's trade portal. All importers and exporters must register here.

Go to: www.cbp.gov/trade/automated

Create an ACE account as Importer or Exporter using your EIN

Complete CBP Form 5106 (Importer Identity Form) if needed

For exports: submit Certification of Authority (COA) on company letterhead to exportreports@census.gov

For export report access by EIN: log into ACE → Accounts tab → Exporter → submit COA

US Import Process

Verify your product's HS (Harmonized Tariff Schedule) code via CBP's database

Check duty rates using the US Harmonized Tariff Schedule

For food: file FDA Prior Notice at least 8 hours before vessel/air arrival

Arrange USDA inspection for meat, plants, and animal products

Submit Customs Entry Summary (CBP Form 7501) within 15 calendar days of arrival

For sea shipments: file 10+2 Importer Security Filing (ISF) at least 24 hours before foreign loading

Goods released within 24–48 hours if no issues; exams or lab tests may delay

Keep all import records for 5+ years as required by CBP

US Export Process

Determine if your product is on the Commerce Control List (CCL) at BIS

If controlled: apply for export license through BIS (www.bis.doc.gov)

If not controlled: most goods qualify for NLR (No License Required) designation

File Electronic Export Information (EEI) through AES for shipments over $2,500

Obtain any product-specific permits (FDA, USDA, EPA, FCC as applicable)

Prepare commercial invoice, packing list, and bill of lading

Submit all documents to your freight forwarder or customs broker

Obtain IEC from DGFT (India) for importing entity
Register with US CBP and obtain EIN
Check India's import policy (Free / Restricted / Prohibited)
Verify India customs duty rates on Customs Tariff Schedule
File Shipping Bill in India's ICEGATE system before export
Ensure BIS certification for electronics entering India
Prepare all shipping docs: invoice, packing list, BOL, COO
Pay applicable customs duties upon arrival in India
Comply with India's FSSAI for food products

Warning

Common Mistakes to Avoid

These errors can result in shipment seizures, heavy fines, or criminal prosecution

Shipping Without IEC

Illegal in India. Goods will be seized at customs and the business can face penalties and blacklisting from DGFT.

Wrong HS Code

Using the incorrect Harmonized System code leads to wrong duty calculations, customs delays, and potential fraud investigation.

Missing ISF Filing (USA)

CBP imposes fines of $5,000 or more per late or missing Importer Security Filing. Must be filed 24 hours before foreign loading.

No Certificate of Origin

Without COO, you cannot claim preferential duty rates under trade agreements. Adds unnecessary import costs.

Skipping ECGC Insurance

Leaves you fully exposed if a foreign buyer defaults on payment. ECGC covers 60–90% of losses from buyer insolvency.

Outdated IEC Profile

Annual profile update (April–June) is mandatory. Failing to update leads to IEC deactivation and inability to ship.

Exporting Controlled Goods Without BIS License

Federal crime in the USA. Can result in $250,000+ fines per violation and up to 20 years imprisonment.

Incorrect Invoice Valuation

Under-invoicing to reduce duties is customs fraud in both India and the USA. Carries severe penalties and shipment seizure.
