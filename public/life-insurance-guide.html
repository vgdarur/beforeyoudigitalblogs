Life Insurance Policy Types

Simple study summary for exam preparation

General Concept

Life insurance transfers the financial risk of premature death from the insured person to the insurance company. It creates an immediate estate for beneficiaries.

Risk Transfer Immediate Estate Death Benefit

1. Term Life Insurance

Term life insurance provides temporary protection for a specific period. It is the simplest and usually the cheapest form of life insurance.

Key Features

  • Temporary coverage only
  • No cash value or equity
  • Lowest initial premium
  • Provides maximum protection for the lowest cost

Common Types

  • Level Term: Death benefit stays the same.
  • Increasing Term: Death benefit increases over time.
  • Decreasing Term: Death benefit decreases over time.
  • Mortgage Redemption: A type of decreasing term policy.
  • Credit Life: Pays off a loan if the debtor dies.
  • Annual Renewable Term: Renewable each year without proof of insurability.

2. Whole Life Insurance

Whole life insurance is permanent insurance that provides lifetime protection with fixed premiums, fixed death benefit, and tax-deferred cash value.

Key Features

  • Permanent lifetime coverage
  • Fixed death benefit
  • Fixed premium
  • Builds cash value
  • Designed to mature at age 100

Types

  • Ordinary Whole Life / Straight Life
  • Limited Payment Whole Life
  • Single Premium Whole Life
  • Modified Whole Life
  • Graded Premium Whole Life
  • Indexed Whole Life

3. Universal Life Insurance

Universal Life is permanent insurance with flexible premiums, adjustable death benefit, and tax-deferred cash value.

  • Flexible premium payments
  • Adjustable death benefit
  • Cash value earns interest
  • Policy may stay active as long as cost of insurance is paid
  • Can be surrendered for cash value

Death Benefit Options

  • Option A: Level death benefit.
  • Option B: Face amount plus cash value.

4. Variable Life Insurance

Variable Life combines life insurance with investment choices. Cash value and death benefit may change based on investment performance.

  • Permanent insurance
  • Fixed premium
  • Cash value invested in securities
  • Policy owner controls investments
  • Regulated by the SEC
  • Higher risk than traditional life insurance

5. Variable Universal Life Insurance

Variable Universal Life combines Universal Life flexibility with Variable Life investment control.

  • Flexible premiums
  • Adjustable death benefit
  • Investment-based cash value
  • Cash value can increase or decrease
  • Combines insurance and securities features

6. Special Use Policies

  • Family Plan: Covers all family members under one policy.
  • Family Maintenance: Combines whole life and level term insurance.
  • Joint Life: Covers two or more people and pays at the first death.
  • Last-to-Die / Survivorship: Pays after the last insured person dies.
  • Juvenile Life: Life insurance on a minor.
  • Endowment: Pays if the insured dies or survives to a specified date.
  • Industrial Life: Small coverage amount with weekly or monthly premiums.

Easy Memory Trick

  • Term = Temporary
  • Whole Life = Whole Lifetime
  • Universal Life = Flexible
  • Variable Life = Investments
  • VUL = Flexible + Investments
  • Endowment = Pays if you live or die

Exam Focus

For the exam, focus mainly on the differences between Term Life, Whole Life, Universal Life, Variable Life, and Variable Universal Life. Pay special attention to cash value, premium structure, death benefit, and whether the policy is temporary or permanent.
